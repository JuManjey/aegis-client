AEGIS is a zero-trust messenger for Android that assumes nothing — not the
network, not the provider, not the transport, not even its own server.

It combines the industry-standard Signal Protocol (PQXDH + Double Ratchet) with a
second encryption layer keyed by a secret that never passes through the server,
and routes all traffic over Tor by default. We do not invent our own
cryptography: every primitive comes from audited libraries.

Key properties
• Multi-layer encryption (defense-in-depth). Each message is wrapped in a
  cascade container (XChaCha20-Poly1305 → AES-256-GCM → Camellia-256 →
  Twofish-256) on a key that is never sent through the server, then sealed again
  by the Signal Double Ratchet. Breaking one of the ciphers or the Signal layer
  does not reveal content. Guard the container pre-shared key (PSK) as your main
  secret: in the current version, whoever learns it can silently join the
  conversation as another device of your contact.
• Tor by default. All client traffic, calls included, is routed through an
  embedded Tor (Arti); the relay is a v3 Onion Service. Bridges and pluggable
  transports (obfs4, snowflake, meek) help bypass censorship. You can turn Tor
  off for speed — the app then warns plainly that the server and your call
  partner will see your IP; encryption stays the same.
• No phone number. Accounts are a cryptographic key pair; sign in with a
  login/password or an auto-generated identity. Recovery is via a mnemonic
  phrase (it restores the account, not the message history).
• Adding contacts. In person, by exchanging QR codes: the PSK never passes
  through the server, so even a fully malicious server cannot MITM you.
  Remotely, by contact address: the shared secret is derived from a signed
  handshake. Mnemonic and NFC exchange do not work yet.
• On-device protection. SQLCipher database, keys in the Android Keystore /
  StrongBox, password (Argon2id) or biometric unlock, screenshot blocking
  (FLAG_SECURE) on every screen except the QR scanner, no cloud backup.
• Encrypted calls. Voice and video over WebRTC/DTLS-SRTP with an additional
  AES-128-GCM frame layer keyed from the Signal channel and the PSK, plus a
  short authentication string (SAS); with Tor on, media goes over Tor too.
• Emergency wipe. Crypto-shredding of all keys (instant) plus best-effort
  overwrite of free space (up to 4 GiB), with an optional duress password that
  wipes data instead of unlocking.
• Auto-deletion. The server keeps messages for at most 7 days; on the device
  you can choose a shorter window.
• Secure defaults (Paranoid profile); read receipts, typing indicators and
  online status are on, but can be turned off in privacy settings.

Honest trade-offs
Out-of-band key exchange adds friction and requires an in-person contact. The
static PSK does not provide forward secrecy on its own (the Signal layer does).
The server cannot read content, but metadata is not fully protected from it:
the relay operator can link one user's mailboxes and reconstruct who talks to
whom (without names), and message type and size are visible to it. Revoking a
linked device is not yet reliable. More layers mean more surface area, so an
independent security audit is mandatory — the project is fully open source
(GPL-3.0-or-later) with reproducible builds to make that audit verifiable.

Status: this is a phase-2 build. It has not yet undergone an external security
audit. Do not rely on it for high-risk use until that audit is complete.
